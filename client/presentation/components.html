Stats chart. Smooth 2.5px orange line with a soft glow and an orange-to-transparent area; quiet dotted grid; tertiary axis labels. Line selected = glowing point, dashed marker. Bar = chart bar (active: orange gradient, others: subtle).

Chart spec
Y axis (scale + unit)

Three dotted gridlines: max, middle, 0, drawn only across the plot. Labels sit in a 40px left gutter (right-aligned, 8px from the plot, vertically centred on their line) in caption/regular, text/tertiary, so they never overlap data. Compact values: €1.5K, €750, €0 for money; 30K, 15K, 0 for counts, with the metric named in the card’s info row (“Views – 21,000”). The max rounds up to a readable number (1, 1.5, 2, 2.5, 3, 5 × 10ⁿ).

X axis (labels)

Line charts: first date of the period on the left (“Sep 12, 2025”), “Today” on the right. Bar charts: one label per bar. F3–F10 in the kit are placeholders only: real data shows the factory short name (max 4 characters, ellipsis), and the selected bar’s full name appears in the “Factory – name” row under the chart.

Selected value (tooltip)

Tap or drag on the chart = Fixated state. Line: orange crosshair + dot, info row reads “date – value + unit” (e.g. “Sep 30 – €320”). Bar: selected bar stays orange, the others go subtle, the row names the factory and its value (“PixelForge – €1,200”). Tapping outside the chart returns to Overall. The selection is never shown by colour alone: there is always a text row.

Accessible data alternative

Each chart is one element with a summary label: title, period, total, highest and lowest point (e.g. “Earnings, Sep 12 to today, total €2,500, highest €320 on Sep 30”). The data points are also exposed as a list (date or factory, value + unit) in reading order. Changing the selection announces the new value. Settings and “View details” stay normal buttons.

    What is clipping, exactly, and how is it not just repackaging?

    Clipping means producing vertical short-form videos (edits, montages, compilations, comparisons) from content that already exists, and publishing them organically on TikTok, Instagram Reels and YouTube Shorts. The work is creative, not mechanical.

    At Klippin, each clip is crafted for a specific audience, with its own angle, its own pacing and its own hook. That is why a hundred clips from one asset are a hundred different clips. Quality comes first: our clippers work closely with our team to make every clip as strong as it can be, in substance and in style.
