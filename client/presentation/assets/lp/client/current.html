Clipping, explained

What it is, how we run it, why it works.

Three answers, before we talk about your campaign.

01

What is clipping?

Clipping turns longer content into short-form videos and puts it back into circulation. A trailer, gameplay, a stream moment, a reaction to it: anything from your world becomes thousands of distinct clips, published across the social platforms by dedicated networks of vetted clippers, on their own accounts. Real edits, made to be watched.

02

How does it work?

You give us the content and the objective. We build the networks around your campaign: clippers already in your space, clippers close to it, or new accounts grown from scratch for your campaign. Every network has a dedicated person on our side who knows its clippers one by one. The clippers publish, and we check every clip against your brief: only the ones that bring real value count and are invoiced. You follow and steer the whole campaign as it runs.

03

Why does it work?

The feed does not reward the biggest budget. It rewards what people choose to watch. Hundreds of clippers testing hundreds of angles travel further than one brand account posting once. Each round of clips sharpens the next, and visibility grows with it. The reach is earned, not bought.

FAQ

Questions, answered.

What is clipping, exactly, and how is it not just repackaging?

Clipping means producing vertical short-form videos (edits, montages, compilations, comparisons) from content that already exists, and publishing them organically on TikTok, Instagram Reels and YouTube Shorts. The work is creative, not mechanical.

At Klippin, each clip is crafted for a specific audience, with its own angle, its own pacing and its own hook. That is why a hundred clips from one asset are a hundred different clips. Quality comes first: our clippers work closely with our team to make every clip as strong as it can be, in substance and in style.

How do you know the views are real?

Every clip is posted by a dedicated, vetted clipper on a real account, and every view is checked first by AI, then by our human team, before it reaches your invoice. Bots and boosted views are detected upstream and filtered downstream. An unverified view is never invoiced. You are not asked to trust a report. You are only ever charged for what survived verification.

How do you protect our brand universe, our rights and our embargoes?

There are four layers to it.

Every clip is reviewed first by AI, then by our human team, against your brief and your brand rules before it counts. Only clips that meet the standard are counted, invoiced and rewarded, so the networks share your interest in getting it right.

Your embargo dates and your spoiler rules are campaign constraints, not suggestions. Confidential assets and dates never leave our team: we prepare the plan and the communication strategy in-house, and clippers only receive content once it is public. Klippin is your single point of trust. Where you require attribution or channel credit, it is part of the clip specification.

Comment sentiment is monitored, and if a clip's discussion turns against you, we move to take it down.

And you can request a takedown at any time. We handle it directly with the clipper, and you never pay for a clip you have refused.

How is this different from influencer marketing, UGC or KOLs?

A KOL deal rents one voice. You pay for a handful of placements on a chosen account at a chosen moment, you reach that account's audience and nothing beyond it, and the post reads as sponsored because it is.

Clipping runs on the opposite logic. Hundreds of clippers, organized into dedicated networks, publish thousands of distinct clips on their own accounts, each one built for a different audience, and the algorithm decides what travels. Instead of buying access to one audience, you put your content into circulation and let the platform find everyone it should reach.

There is also a difference in what you get back. UGC hands you assets that you then have to distribute yourself. Here the clip is made and published by the same person, in the format their audience already watches, so creation and distribution happen in one move.

How does this compare to paid, and do we have to choose?

No, and we would not advise it. Paid puts a chosen asset in front of a chosen audience at a chosen moment; clipping works through iteration and volume, and lets the audience decide what travels. They do different jobs, and clipping tends to make paid work harder. Clipping doesn't replace paid: it extends it.

What platforms do the clips go out on?

TikTok, Instagram Reels and YouTube Shorts. Those three carry short-form discovery, and their recommendation engines are strong enough to take a clip far beyond the account that posted it. Your campaign can run on just one of them or on all three: the choice depends on your objectives, your audience and your target market, and it evolves with the performance we see.

What do you need from us, and how much work is it on our side?

Content and an objective.

Send us your assets, whether that is a trailer, gameplay, a stream or an announce clip. Tell us what the campaign is for and when your window opens, and give us your rules: what is off-limits, what is under embargo, what needs crediting. We turn that into the distribution plan, covering angles, formats, target audiences, cadence and brand-safety guardrails.

Everything after that is on us. We build your dedicated networks, review every clip, coordinate the clippers and report to you every week. This is not a marketplace you have to operate. You brief us once, and steer whenever you want.

Can we steer the campaign once it's live?

Yes, and that is how it is designed to run. Thousands of clips go out, and the data shows which angles, formats and moments land with which audiences. We push more of what works and drop what doesn't, round after round. You see it as it happens, and you can steer the topics and angles at any time: a new announcement to push, a narrative to shift, a moment to ride. And the data comes back to you: what works, for which audience, in a weekly report ready to feed your paid, your community and your next release.

How long between first contact and first clips live?

Your dedicated networks are activated within seven days. Once we have your assets and your objectives, we build the distribution plan, select the clippers for your networks and share the campaign guidelines with them as soon as your content is cleared to go public. Publication then follows, and your distribution plan states the ramp-up we commit to against your release window.

What decides the size of the budget?

It follows the reach you are targeting, the number of markets in scope, and the length of the window. Those three inputs are what we work through with you, and the outcome is a quoted figure in your distribution plan, not a rate card applied to a brief we have not read yet.

What happens when the budget is reached, or isn't fully spent?

Your budget is a ceiling and it holds. Once it is reached, billing stops, but your clips don't: they stay online and keep generating views you don't pay for. There is no overage, no additional invoice, and no view charged that has not been verified.

If the campaign closes without consuming the whole budget, we settle the remainder with you directly, based on the campaign and on what is most useful to you next. We agree it when we build the distribution plan, so it is not something you discover on the invoice.
